# Pedagogical Authoring Guide

## Teaching Flow Principles

A lesson presentation must have clear pedagogical momentum:

1. **Title & Onboarding**: Establish the big picture and generate interest.
2. **Roadmap**: Lay out the progression of ideas so students see how concepts interconnect.
3. **Learning Objectives**: Frame goals using active Bloom's verbs (Define, Calculate, Compare, Apply).
4. **Prior Knowledge & Warm-Up**: Activate prerequisite knowledge before introducing new complexity.
5. **Core Definition**: Explicit, formal definitions with high visual contrast.
6. **Visual Explanation**: Models, diagrams, and processes showing mechanisms.
7. **Worked Example**: Multi-step problems broken into numbered cards with progressive reveal animations.
8. **Common Misconception**: Actively address tempting traps: show the misconception crossed out in red, alongside the verified correct reasoning in green.
9. **Formative Checks (2-Stage Pattern)**:
   - **Slide A (Prompt)**: Question only with thinking time instructions. No answer given.
   - **Slide B (Reveal)**: Correct answer highlighted, detailed pedagogical reasoning, distractor analysis.
10. **Recap & Consolidation**: 3-4 golden takeaways summarizing fundamental laws.
11. **Practice & Exam Questions**: Independent exercises for desk work or homework.
12. **Exit Ticket**: Rapid end-of-class reflection questions to evaluate mastery.

## Speaker Notes Architecture

Every slide includes rich speaker notes divided into six sections:
- **Teacher Explanation**: What the teacher can say aloud to clarify the slide content.
- **Emphasis**: Crucial distinctions or formulas students must write down.
- **Ask Students**: Oral Socratic questions to prompt active classroom participation.
- **Likely Misconception**: How students might misinterpret the concept.
- **Transition**: Smooth verbal bridge to the subsequent slide.
- **Optional Extension**: Advanced insight or application if class time permits.
